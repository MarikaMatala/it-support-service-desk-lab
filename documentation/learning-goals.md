# Learning Goals

This document defines the skills I want to develop through the IT Support & Service Desk Learning Lab. It is a learning roadmap, not a claim of professional experience or expertise.

The goals may change as I complete exercises and discover which topics need more practice. I will document practical evidence in individual exercise notes and update my progress honestly.

## 1. Service Desk Fundamentals

**Goals**
- Understand the purpose and daily workflow of an IT service desk.
- Practise recording incidents and service requests in a clear, structured way.
- Learn how to gather relevant information before attempting a fix.
- Understand ticket prioritisation, impact, urgency, escalation and service-level agreements (SLAs).
- Practise communicating technical information clearly and professionally to different users.
- Recognise when an issue should be escalated rather than continuing without sufficient access or knowledge.

**Evidence of progress**
- Documented simulated tickets or support scenarios.
- Clear problem descriptions, troubleshooting notes and resolution summaries.
- Examples of prioritisation and escalation decisions.

## 2. Troubleshooting and Problem Solving

**Goals**
- Develop a repeatable troubleshooting process: identify, investigate, form a hypothesis, test, resolve and verify.
- Separate symptoms from likely root causes.
- Change one variable at a time where practical and record the results.
- Use error messages, logs and system information as evidence.
- Verify that a solution works and consider whether it could cause side effects.
- Improve the ability to explain both the cause of an issue and the reasoning behind a solution.

**Evidence of progress**
- Troubleshooting logs showing tests, observations and outcomes.
- Exercises where the initial hypothesis was revised based on evidence.
- Verification steps and concise root-cause summaries.

## 3. Operating Systems and End-User Support

**Goals**
- Strengthen practical familiarity with Windows operating systems and common desktop support tasks.
- Build basic familiarity with Linux and its command-line environment.
- Practise investigating user accounts, permissions, applications, processes, storage, updates and connectivity.
- Learn to use relevant system settings, administrative tools and command-line utilities safely.
- Understand common causes of login, software installation, performance and peripheral issues.

**Evidence of progress**
- Completed operating-system support exercises.
- Notes explaining commands and administrative tools used.
- Documented checks and verification results.

## 4. Networking Fundamentals

**Goals**
- Understand the roles of IP addresses, subnet masks, default gateways, DNS and DHCP.
- Learn how devices communicate within a local network and access external networks.
- Practise using diagnostic tools such as `ping`, `tracert` / `traceroute`, `ipconfig` / `ifconfig` and `nslookup`.
- Learn to distinguish local connectivity, name-resolution and routing problems.
- Develop a basic understanding of wired and wireless network troubleshooting.

**Evidence of progress**
- Network exercises with recorded command output or redacted screenshots.
- Explanations of what each diagnostic test establishes.
- Troubleshooting notes that identify the likely network layer or component involved.

## 5. Identity, Accounts and Access Management

**Goals**
- Understand the basic concepts behind user accounts, groups, authentication and authorisation.
- Learn the purpose of directory services, including Microsoft Active Directory.
- Practise simulated account and group management tasks where the lab environment allows it.
- Understand file and folder permissions and the principle of least privilege.
- Learn why account lifecycle processes and access reviews matter.

**Evidence of progress**
- Documented identity and access management exercises.
- Explanations of group membership and permission decisions.
- Examples demonstrating least-privilege reasoning.

## 6. Cybersecurity and Safe Support Practices

**Goals**
- Recognise common security risks relevant to everyday IT support.
- Understand phishing, malware, weak credentials, social engineering and unsafe downloads.
- Learn the purpose of multi-factor authentication, updates, backups and endpoint protection.
- Understand basic security principles such as least privilege, defence in depth and data minimisation.
- Practise handling suspicious incidents responsibly, including when and how to escalate them.
- Avoid exposing passwords, tokens, personal information or other sensitive data in notes and screenshots.

**Evidence of progress**
- Security-focused exercises and reflections.
- Documented risk observations and safe response steps.
- Examples of redacted evidence and privacy-conscious documentation.

## 7. Technical Documentation and Knowledge Sharing

**Goals**
- Write clear, structured and reproducible troubleshooting notes.
- Explain technical terms and procedures in language appropriate to the intended reader.
- Record the environment, symptoms, steps taken, results and final outcome.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions.
- Create useful instructions that another person could follow.
- Keep documentation concise, accurate and updated when new information becomes available.

**Evidence of progress**
- Completed exercise reports using the project template.
- Step-by-step guides and troubleshooting checklists.
- Improvements made to earlier documentation based on review.

## 8. Professional Communication and User Experience

**Goals**
- Practise asking focused questions to understand a user's issue.
- Communicate progress, expected next steps and limitations clearly.
- Adapt explanations to the user's technical knowledge.
- Maintain a calm, respectful and service-oriented approach when investigating problems.
- Consider accessibility and usability when giving instructions or recommending solutions.

**Evidence of progress**
- Reflections on simulated user-support scenarios.
- Examples of clear, user-oriented explanations.
- Notes describing how communication could be improved.

## 9. Learning Process and Progress Tracking

For each exercise, I aim to:
1. State what I am trying to learn.
2. Record the starting situation and relevant constraints.
3. Explain my approach and why I chose it.
4. Record tests, commands, observations and unsuccessful attempts where relevant.
5. Verify the result instead of assuming the issue is resolved.
6. Reflect on what I learned and what I would investigate next.

I will record the time actually spent on an exercise. I will not estimate or invent time, results or experience to make progress appear greater than it is.

### Progress scale

I can use this scale when reviewing each learning area:

| Level | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Not started | I have not yet explored this topic. |
| Exploring | I understand some basic concepts and am beginning practical exercises. |
| Practising | I can complete guided or familiar tasks and explain the main steps. |
| Developing independence | I can investigate common scenarios with limited guidance and verify my work. |
| Confident in the lab | I can handle a range of lab scenarios independently and explain my decisions. |

These levels describe my learning progress in practice or simulated environments. They do not represent professional certification or workplace experience.

## 10. Overall Objective

My overall objective is to build a practical foundation in IT support, service desk workflows and technical troubleshooting. I want to become better at investigating problems systematically, communicating clearly, documenting solutions and understanding the systems that users rely on.

This project is an independent learning lab. Exercises completed in simulators or personal test environments will be identified as such and will not be presented as professional work experience.
